# Hazard log

What can go wrong for a family using Care Circle, how it would show up, what catches it, and the test that fails if the catch breaks. It is written the way medical-device teams write it (in the spirit of ISO 14971), without claiming to be one: this is a record-keeping tool, it is not a medical device, and nothing here has been reviewed by a clinician.

`tests/test_hazard_log.py` reads this file. Every row must name at least one test as `tests/<file>.py::<test name>`, and each named test must exist. A hazard with no test cannot be added.

The review that prompted this log observed that the product guarded against the *model saying something bad* while the real hazards were **silent omissions**: an ingredient dropped without a word, a medicine that could not be entered, a verified badge on a wrong strength. The first rows are those.

## Not caught (stated, not hidden)

- **A wrong or out-of-date brand list.** `carecircle/brands.py` is hand-written and unverified; a manufacturer can change a formulation. A brand that is not in the list is never reduced to one ingredient, but a listed brand with a changed formulation would show the old ingredients. The note on every combination proposal says "check the pack".
- **A medicine entered that does not exist at all.** It is recorded as "not checked"; nothing says it is a real medicine.
- **A carer who marks a dose given that was not given.** The app records what people say. It cannot know.
- **A pharmacist link that was forwarded.** Whoever holds the link can read it until it expires or is closed; the owner sees how many times it was opened, not by whom.
- **A reminder whose every channel keeps failing.** After five tries it is left used; nobody is told it was dropped. The Circle screen still shows the dose as not marked.
- **Cookie sessions are off on the live site.** Until `CC_COOKIE_SESSIONS=1` is set there, the session token sits in the browser's storage and an injected script could read it; the page has no known injection, and React escapes what it renders, but that is the residual risk.
- **A hard crash of the database loses up to the last minute of writes** (it keeps snapshots only). The pre-deploy, 6-hourly and daily copies sit behind that.
- **The site-down alarm tells nobody until an address is subscribed to its topic.** It is visible in the CloudWatch console either way.
- **Free text can still name someone.** A name on a line the identity filter does not recognise (a name written after a medicine, in the middle of a sentence) goes to the model with the rest of the text. The helper masks the names it knows; the label reader removes whole lines that start with a name, age, address, phone or id word.
- **Interaction data that is wrong or missing.** DDInter has a quarter of its pairs without a severity, and the enzyme layer touches 32 of 1,833 medicines from clinical tables, 63 counting laboratory findings (README, "Limits").
- **Real-world accuracy of photo and voice reading.** Measured on synthetic labels and a handful of real photos only (`docs/BENCHMARKS.md`, B4 and B4b).
